## 2. EXPLORATORY QUESTIONS

### Guiding Questions:

What are the main questions driving your research?

What sub-questions will help you delve deeper into your main research question?

How can these questions guide your search for information and evidence?

Write at least 10 questions using complete sentences.

### Answer

**Main research question:**

How much do people in our local community know about climate and environmental news, common waste types, and keeping their surroundings clean, and how does that knowledge relate to what they do?

**Questions for residents:**

1. In the past seven days, have you heard or read any climate or environmental news?
2. Where did you hear or read it: radio, television, social media, community meeting, or another source?
3. Which language do you usually use for climate and environmental information: Kinyarwanda, English, or another language?
4. Which of these environmental issues have you heard about or noticed in Rwanda?
5. Looking at these pictures, which items can break down naturally: food scraps, leaves, plastic, glass, or metal?
6. Which of these actions help keep your home and surroundings clean?
7. In the past seven days, what did your household do with its rubbish?
8. Does your household separate any rubbish before disposal?
9. Which waste collection, composting, or recycling services have you used?
10. What makes it difficult for your household to keep the surroundings clean or dispose of rubbish properly?

We shall read each question aloud in Kinyarwanda or English, as preferred, and use picture cards for waste questions. We shall offer simple answer choices, including “do not know” where appropriate, and record responses as numbers. We shall not suggest answers to residents.

## 4. EVIDENCE

### Guiding Questions:

What types of statistical data are necessary to support your research findings?

Are there significant trends or patterns in the data that you need to identify?

Which quotes or expert opinions will add credibility and depth to your research?

### Answer

We shall collect numerical answers from 40 adult residents in our local community. We shall visit households along a consistent route through different parts of the community, invite one adult per household, and continue until 40 residents have taken part. We shall record how many people we invite, how many take part, and how many decline. The findings shall describe the people who take part and shall not be presented as figures for all of Rwanda.

We shall ask the same questions before and after the action period. We shall also choose three shared public places and count visible rubbish in the same marked areas at each place, before and after. We shall record the type and number of items without handling them.

We shall record:

1. The number of residents invited, participating, and declining.
2. How many residents heard or read environmental news in the previous seven days, and the sources and languages they reported.
3. The number of correct answers to simple environmental-knowledge questions.
4. The number and percentage of waste items residents correctly identify from picture cards.
5. The number of residents who can name practical ways to keep their surroundings clean.
6. The number and percentage of households reporting waste separation and each common disposal method.
7. The number of residents reporting barriers to proper waste disposal or getting environmental information.
8. The number of visible rubbish items counted at each observation place.
9. Changes in residents’ answers and rubbish counts between the first and second measurements.

We shall enter answers in a tally sheet, calculate totals and percentages, and compare the first and second measurements. We shall also compare knowledge answers with reported household practices. We shall state how many people answered each question and note unanswered questions. Since this is a small study without a comparison group, we shall report changes we observe without claiming that one activity alone caused them.

## 5. RISKS & CHALLENGES

### Guiding Questions:

What potential risks or challenges might you face during your research?

How will you mitigate or address these risks and challenges?

What backup plans do you have if things do not go as expected?

### Answer

| Risk or challenge | How we shall address it |
|---|---|
| Residents may refuse to participate. | We shall explain the study, make participation voluntary, and record refusals without names. We shall not pressure anyone. |
| Residents may fear that their answers could affect them. | We shall explain that answers are private and will not affect local services. We shall speak to residents privately where possible. |
| Residents may give inaccurate answers or say what they think we want to hear. | We shall use simple, neutral questions and compare answers with the picture questions and rubbish counts. |
| Residents may share false information about climate or the environment. | We shall record answers as residents’ views and check factual information against trusted sources such as REMA, NISR, and IPCC. |
| Residents may not understand some waste terms. | We shall use familiar examples and pictures, and try the questions with a few residents before the survey. |
| Some residents may not read or write easily. | We shall read questions aloud in the resident’s preferred language and record the answers ourselves. |
| Some residents may be away when we visit. | We shall visit at different times and return where possible. |
| Community leaders may affect who agrees to take part. | We shall request access respectfully, then invite residents to answer privately and voluntarily. |
| Residents may feel blamed for how they dispose of rubbish. | We shall use respectful questions and make clear that we are studying community conditions, not judging individuals. |
| National figures may not describe our local community. | We shall label national figures clearly and use our own collected results for local findings. |
| Rain, collection days, or community events may change the rubbish count. | We shall count the same places in the same way and note conditions that may affect the count. |
| Names or answers may become identifiable. | We shall use numbers instead of names, keep completed forms safely, and not publish identifying details. |
| Researchers may record answers differently. | We shall use the same questions and tally sheets and check records together each day. |
| Rubbish may contain dangerous items. | We shall count from a safe place and shall not touch or move any rubbish. |
| Forms may be incomplete or lost. | We shall check forms after each visit, store them safely, and report missing answers without guessing. |
| We may not have enough time to reach everyone. | We shall visit different parts of the local community and state clearly how many people took part. |
